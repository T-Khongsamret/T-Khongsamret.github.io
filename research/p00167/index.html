01 / RESEARCH RECORD

Abstract

This paper compares predefined single-phase-shift dual-active-bridge (SPS-DAB) and asymmetric CLLC bidirectional dc-dc converters in PSIM. The study covers a 400-V dc link, 250-450-V battery voltage, 1-6-kW power, and both grid-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-grid directions. Sixty operating-point pairs, each containing one record per converter, satisfy common numerical qualification criteria. In the baseline modeled-efficiency map, the CLLC nominally leads 31 pairs (27 of 30 at 1-3 kW), whereas the SPS-DAB nominally leads 29 (26 of 30 at 4-6 kW, 19 of them by less than 0.25 pp). However, 33 orderings would reverse if no more than 10 W of hypothetical differential omitted loss were assigned to the nominal winner. Model-based screening classifies 12 pairs as supported, 44 as warning, and four as requiring additional validation. An ideal SPS reconstruction matches simulated DAB primary rms current within 1.8% at all 60 points. At three interpretable operating points, replacing the CLLC parameterized bridges with explicit MOSFETs reduced included loss by 3.37-13.58 W and reversed one near tie. These local checks do not bound model-form uncertainty across the full operating map. The results support conditional screening of the evaluated implementations, not a universal or hardware-validated topology ranking.

02 / RESEARCH RECORD

Methods and operating envelope

SPS-DAB uses single-phase-shift control at 100 kHz. The asymmetric CLLC uses a predefined 90/95/98/100-kHz schedule and power-dependent duty. Equal external conditions do not establish equal optimization opportunity.

04 / RESEARCH RECORD

Evidence and limitations

Model screening classifies 12 pairs as supported, 44 as warning and four as requiring further validation. Thirty-three rankings are sensitive to hypothetical omitted differential loss of 10 W or less.

DAB uses discrete MOSFETs, CLLC parameterized bridges. Limited explicit-MOSFET checks do not validate the full map. Inductor parasitic losses, gate-loop and thermal effects remain incomplete.
