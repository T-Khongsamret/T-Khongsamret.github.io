FEATURED RESEARCH

A closer look at bidirectional EV charging

Comparing two isolated DC–DC converter architectures across operating conditions, with transparent attention to modeled losses and limitations.

CONFERENCE PAPER • P00167

Comparative Evaluation of DAB and CLLC Bidirectional DC–DC Converters for Wide-Voltage-Range V2G Chargers

A simulation-based comparison of Dual Active Bridge (DAB) and CLLC resonant converters for grid-to-vehicle (G2V) and vehicle-to-grid (V2G) energy transfer. The study examines efficiency, current stress, and loss trends over battery-voltage and power operating points using PSIM. Findings are subject to the modeled components, control methods, and stated validation boundaries.

HOW I WORK

From converter models to evidence

My research focuses on clear operating envelopes, reproducible simulation, and cautious interpretation of performance differences.

01

Converter comparison

Analyze DAB under single-phase-shift control and asymmetric CLLC under phase-shift / frequency-based control within the defined study conditions.

02

Efficiency and losses

Evaluate power transfer, electrical stress, and modeled loss components rather than relying on headline efficiency alone.

03

Toward hardware

Extend the simulation research toward current sensing, protection, PCB design, and measurement-based experimental verification.

ABOUT

I study Electrical and Electronics Engineering at the Faculty of Engineering at Sriracha, Kasetsart University, Thailand. My interests include high-frequency converters, electric vehicle charging, converter control, and practical power-electronics measurement.
